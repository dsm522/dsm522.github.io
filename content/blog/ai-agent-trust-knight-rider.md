I was watching the first episode of Knight Rider and found myself thinking about AI agents.

Which is not where I expected my brain to go, but here we are.

Michael Knight starts out wary of KITT: a talking car that watches, reasons, warns, suggests, and occasionally saves your life is not something you immediately accept as normal.

But it is not the tech that is interesting. It is the trust.

Over time, Michael does not become less human because KITT is there. He does not surrender his judgement. He still chooses the mission, takes the risks, makes the calls, and lives with the consequences.

But he is no longer alone in quite the same way.

There is a bond that forms when something is consistently there to help. When it remembers what matters. When it notices what you missed. When it turns up at the right moment, not to take over, but to stand alongside you.

I think that is the part of AI agents we do not talk about enough.

Yes, we talk about productivity, automation, workflows and efficiency. But there is something more human underneath it.

Dependence is not always weakness. Sometimes it is what happens when trust has been earned.

The question is whether that dependence preserves our agency or quietly erodes it.

For me, the best version of AI is not a machine making decisions on our behalf. It is a faithful companion in the work. One that helps us see further, move faster, remember better, and still leaves the final choice where it belongs.

With the human.
